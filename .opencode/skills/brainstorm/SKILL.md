# brainstorm

One mind brainstorming alone keeps landing on the same obvious ideas. So think from five very
different points of view, separately, then pick the best.

## 1. Get the topic

Take the subject and any limits (who it's for, budget, tone, things to avoid) from their message.
Don't interrogate — only ask one quick question if there is no direction at all.

## 2. Five thinkers

If you have the `task` tool, start five `general` helpers **at the same time**, one per thinker
below. Give each the topic, the limits, its thinking style, and this instruction:

> Give 4 ideas only someone with your mindset would come up with. Skip the obvious ones. For each:
> a short title, then one or two sentences on what it is. No questions, no intro — just the list.

If you can't start helpers, play each thinker yourself, one after another, and don't let one
thinker's ideas leak into the next.

| Thinker | Asks |
|---|---|
| **The Practical One** | What could we start this week with what we already have? |
| **The Dreamer** | Forget the limits — what's the 10× version? |
| **The Rebel** | What is everyone assuming that's wrong? What if we did the opposite? |
| **The People Person** | What does the person using this actually feel and want? |
| **The Borrower** | How do games, nature or a totally different industry solve this? |

## 3. Pick the best five

From the ~20 ideas, choose five that are **strong and different from each other**. Merge
near-duplicates. At least one should be a bold, surprising pick.

Show them as a numbered list: title, one line on what it is, one line on why it made the cut. Then
ask which one they want to take further — and suggest `/spec` for it.
