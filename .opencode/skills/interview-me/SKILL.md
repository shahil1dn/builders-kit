# interview-me

## When to use it

- They named a thing to make but not who it is for or what it should do for them.
- Two readings of the request would lead to very different results.
- You are about to guess.

If the goal is already clear, skip this and get on with it — and don't announce that you skipped.

## How

**One question at a time.** Wait for the answer before asking the next.

Ask about **what they want to happen**, never about how it is built:

- Good: "When someone lands on your page, what's the one thing they should do?"
- Bad: "Should this be React or plain HTML?" — that is your decision, not theirs.

Offer **two or three concrete choices** instead of an open question. "Is this for your friends, for
customers, or just for you?" gets a quick answer. "Who is it for?" often gets "I don't know".

## Stop when

You could describe what success looks like in one short paragraph, in words they would use. Then
say it back to them and ask: "Is that right?" Once they say yes, suggest `/spec` or `/brainstorm`.
